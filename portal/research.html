Research

CardioSense is also a small study. Here is the question, what we found, and what it does not show.

Who needs the extra tests?

The most accurate heart-disease risk estimate needs a stress test, fluoroscopy and a nuclear scan. A first-visit estimate needs none of them but is less accurate. Can the model's own uncertainty decide which patients can skip the extra tests, and what does that cost?

0.91AUC with all 13 inputs (95% CI 0.87 to 0.94)
0.84AUC from first-visit inputs only (0.79 to 0.88)
34%of patients can skip the extra tests for a 1.5 point accuracy loss
60%can skip them for a 3.0 point loss

What we learned

  • Seven other model families, including forests, boosting and an SVM, did not beat plain logistic regression by more than noise.
  • With 139 disease cases, the full model is only just large enough by a standard sample-size rule. That is why complex models did not help.
  • A conformal wrapper gives a guarantee about how often the confident calls are right. In this two-class problem it picks the same patients as a simple probability band, so the wrapper adds trust, not accuracy.

What this does not show

  • It was tested only inside one clinic from the 1980s. No other hospital, no prospective study.
  • 46% of these patients had disease because they were referred for angiography. The percentages are far too high for the general public.
  • Only 25 women had disease, so results for women are imprecise.
  • It does not show that using the tool prevents heart attacks. That needs a clinical study.

Method in brief

Logistic regression on 13 clinical inputs and on the 7 first-visit inputs. Twenty repeats of five-fold cross-validation with bootstrap intervals, a bootstrap optimism correction, a sample-size check, decision-curve analysis and split-conformal prediction with the calibration set held inside each training fold. Every number on this page comes from a script with fixed seeds.

Data: UCI Heart Disease, Cleveland (Janosi, Steinbrunn, Pfisterer, Detrano 1988; CC BY 4.0; doi.org/10.24432/C52P4X). Educational prototype, not a medical device. Terms
